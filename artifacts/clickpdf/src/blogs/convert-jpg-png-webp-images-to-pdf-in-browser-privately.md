## 2. Understanding Digital Image Formats: JPG vs. PNG vs. WebP

To get a suitable PDF output, it helps to understand how common image formats store visual information. The **Image to PDF** tool accepts JPG/JPEG, PNG, and WebP images:

### JPG / JPEG (Joint Photographic Experts Group)
JPG is a common format for digital-camera and smartphone photography. It uses "lossy" compression algorithms to shrink file sizes by selectively discarding microscopic color variations that the human eye rarely notices. 
* **Best suited for:** Smartphone photos of paper receipts, scanned physical contracts, real estate photography, and full-color product catalogs.
* **Why convert JPG to PDF:** While JPGs are easy to capture on a phone, emailing ten separate camera photos results in inconsistent zoom levels when opened by a client. Wrapping them inside a PDF standardizes the viewing frame.

### PNG (Portable Network Graphics)
PNG is a lossless raster format that supports transparency. It preserves the encoded pixel values, though it cannot restore detail that was missing or blurry in the original image.
* **Best suited for:** Software screenshots, digital UI mockups, company logos, architectural diagrams, charts, and scanned text documents with high contrast.
* **Why convert PNG to PDF:** PNG screenshots can be combined into one multi-page document, which is useful for creating a software guide, visual record, or presentation handout.

### WebP (Modern Web Picture Format)
WebP is a modern image format that can provide efficient lossy or lossless compression; results vary with the image and encoding settings. Whenever you save an image from a modern website, an e-commerce store, or a digital workspace, it frequently downloads with a `.webp` extension.
* **Best suited for:** Web research archives, saved digital invoices, online infographics, and modern marketing assets.
* **Why convert WebP to PDF:** Some older programs do not support WebP. Converting a supported WebP image to PDF can make it easier to share with recipients who use common PDF viewers, though you should check the result on the target device.

## 3. Why Combine Images into a PDF

Many organizations prefer or require PDFs for submissions and records. Combining images into one PDF can offer practical benefits, although the recipient's requirements should always be checked:

### A. More Consistent Sharing and Printing
When you send a raw image file to a colleague, the way they see it depends entirely on their default photo viewer, their monitor calibration, and their operating system. A PDF records page dimensions and content placement, which can make sharing more consistent across devices. Rendering can still vary slightly by viewer, printer, fonts, and display settings.

### B. Preserving Sequential Narrative Order
Imagine photographing an eight-page signed lease agreement with your phone. If you attach those eight JPGs to an email or upload them to a messaging app, network upload speeds often scramble the order—placing Page 5 before Page 1. When you use **clickpdf.net** to convert multiple image files into a single clean PDF, you lock the exact page sequence permanently so the reader experiences the document from start to finish.

### C. Eliminating Zip-Folder Friction
When sharing several images, a single multi-page PDF may be easier to preview than a ZIP archive. Email and submission systems have their own file-size and file-type rules, so check the recipient's requirements before sending.

### D. More Predictable Printing
Printing raw images directly from a browser or photo app may produce unexpected scaling or margins. A PDF lets you choose page dimensions and image placement, but inspect the document and printer settings before printing.

### E. Access to Document-Level Security and Metadata
Standalone JPG, PNG, and WebP files generally do not provide PDF-style password protection or document metadata fields. ClickPDF's Protect PDF tool creates a ClickPDF-specific encrypted package with AES-256-GCM rather than a standard password-encrypted PDF; use the matching Unlock PDF workflow to restore the original.
